Please consider “Cross-Source Completion of Historical Chinese Incense Formulas: A Provenance-Blocked Benchmark with Source-Stable Co-Formulation Graphs” for publication as an Article in the Computing and Artificial Intelligence section.

The study asks how an incomplete historical formula can be completed using evidence from other books. Its principal contribution is a provenance-blocked computational benchmark that separates source collections, attributed works and copied formula families. On 91 reviewed formulas from six work groups, conditional co-formulation models improve work-averaged hidden-ingredient F1 from 0.378 to 0.426 over a frequency baseline. The manuscript reports work-level randomisation inference, calibration and the contribution of rare materials to prediction error.

The mathematical development includes a source-stable graph with an interval-robust interpretation, exact beta log-odds moments, a noise-adjusted estimate of source variation, and a constrained information projection for reconstructing proportions while preserving observed ratios. A separate operational evaluation withholds both the true observation fraction and visible mass. The release includes code, source-linked data, experiment outputs, editable figures and a web implementation of the tested model.

The paper also reports results that constrain its interpretation: the source-heterogeneity penalty gives little accuracy benefit with six domains, and unreviewed records do not improve the matched-mask benchmark. These results make the application useful as a reproducible study of source transfer and incomplete compositional records, rather than an unsupported claim of recovering unique historical originals.

The accompanying package contains the manuscript and reproducibility materials. Generative-AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript. Required originality, exclusive-submission, funding and conflict declarations will be confirmed by the author in the submission system.
